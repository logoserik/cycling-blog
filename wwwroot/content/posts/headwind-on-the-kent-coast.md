The forecast promised a light breeze and lied. From the harbour at Whitstable the wind came straight down the seawall, salty and hard, and every flag on the oyster sheds pointed back the way I had come. I dropped into the small ring before the first groynes and stayed there, watching the brown water slap the concrete while the front wheel wandered in the gusts.

Past Seasalter the path narrows and the gravel gets loose. I stopped once to eat a squashed banana and take a picture of the empty beach huts, their paint peeling in the same colours as my frame tape. A dog walker shouted that the tide was still running and that the slipway at Reculver would be wet. It was. I walked the bike across the slick stones, then climbed back on where the sea wall turns inland toward the railway.

![Beach huts beside the seawall](/uploads/kent-coast-ride.jpg)

The return over the marshes felt like a different sport. With the wind behind me the same gear that had been a grind became a spin, and the reed beds hissed instead of fought. I finished at the chip shop by the harbour, shoes chalky with salt, and wrote the loop down before I forgot which turn I had missed near the boatyard. It was not a fast ride. It was a loud one, and the photograph of those beach huts is the part I wanted to keep.
