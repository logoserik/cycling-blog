I rolled out of Dorking while the street lamps were still brighter than the sky. The air on the Zig Zag was cold enough that my breath fogged the lens of my glasses, and the only other sound was a milk float somewhere below the trees. I kept the first lap in the small ring, counting pedal strokes instead of chasing the Strava ghosts who would arrive after sunrise.

By the second lap the chalk had a pale stripe of light on it. A rider in a faded yellow cape came past without a word, then sat up at the cattle grid and waited. We swapped turns for two more efforts, talking only at the top about tyre pressures and whether the espresso at the kiosk was worth the queue. It was.

![Dawn light on the Zig Zag](/uploads/box-hill-dawn.jpg)

I took that photo on the wall before the car park filled, gloves off, hands shaking more from the cold than from the climb. The descent back toward Westhumble was still damp in the shade, so I feathered the brakes and let the morning stay quiet a little longer. Four laps, one bun, and the kind of ride that does not need a story beyond the hill itself. I checked the computer at the cattle grid one last time, then spun home with the sun finally on my back.
